FrictionGraph

A graph-based FX research system for detecting structural market imbalance from triangular currency friction. The project reconstructs latest-known tick states, scores directed currency loops, and turns daily chain-width behavior into interpretable anomaly cases.

What it measures

FrictionGraph treats currency pairs as directed tradeable graph edges. For each analysis minute, it finds the latest quote before that time, constructs triangular loops, and measures how far those loops drift from internal consistency.

Why it matters

A single spike can be noise. A day where multiple chains widen together is more informative. The system focuses on chain-level structure, which helps separate stale quotes, holiday liquidity regimes, and policy-driven repricing events.

Method

From raw tick data to daily structural width.

  1. Ingest raw Dukascopy ticks Download bid/ask tick data by instrument and save chunked Parquet files.
  2. Prepare DuckDB views Expose the Parquet data lake as a queryable fx_ticks view.
  3. Reconstruct latest-known states At every analysis minute, select the latest available quote for each instrument.
  4. Score directed triangular chains Build currency graph loops and normalize scores into friction form.
  5. Aggregate daily width Export sum_rmsd, gap, and chain-day features for Tableau.

Findings

Two recurring classes emerged after the 2025 extension.

Holiday liquidity regime

Good Friday repeats the same structural pattern.

The 2024-03-29 and 2025-04-18 peaks both land on Good Friday and both show a CHF-SGD-centered chain block. This suggests a recurring holiday liquidity signature rather than a one-off anomaly.

Policy repricing regime

JPY-centered events behave differently.

The 2025-12-01 peak is driven by JPY-linked chain volatility, with SGD, CHF, CAD, EUR, and USD rotating around the repricing event. It is a broad intraday instability case, not simply a holiday pattern.

Model interpretation

Top RMSD chain is the right event lens.

Since the overall dashboard is centered on daily sum_rmsd, the explanatory route for a selected day should be the highest-RMSD chain rather than the most negative mean-friction chain.

Case Study

Understand market structure, not arbitrage.

  1. Christmas liquidity peaks 2024-12-25 / 2025-12-25
    Christmas produces the largest daily width peak in both 2024 and 2025. The repetition suggests a holiday liquidity regime rather than a single news-driven anomaly.
  2. Good Friday overlap 2024-03-29 / 2025-04-18
    The 2024-03-29 and 2025-04-18 Good Friday peaks both appear in nearly the same region of the Width vs Chain Gap view, with a similar CHF-SGD-centered structure.
  3. JPY policy repricing 2025-11-28 - 2025-12-01
    Unlike the holiday cases, the 2025-12-01 event appears in 2025 but not in 2024, which helps separate it from recurring calendar effects. The chain structure is JPY-centered, consistent with market reaction to Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda signaling that a December rate hike would be considered. Reuters coverage.

Future Direction

From daily FX friction research to broader market intelligence.

FrictionGraph started from a simple research idea: if market structure becomes stressed, the inconsistency should appear somewhere in the graph before it becomes a clean narrative. Using open Dukascopy FX tick data and daily-resolution features, the project already surfaced a real event pattern, including recurring holiday liquidity regimes and a JPY-centered policy repricing episode.

The next version can improve in two directions. First, the data layer can expand beyond FX into futures, crypto, and equity markets, so the graph captures cross-asset stress instead of currency loops alone. Second, the monitoring frequency can move from daily analysis toward millisecond-level tracking, making the system closer to a real-time market structure monitor.

In practice, this system can act as an event filter. Event-driven strategies often start from news, but the world produces too many headlines to treat every story as actionable. FrictionGraph offers a market-side screen: it helps identify which events actually disturb the trading graph. The same idea can also support risk monitoring by measuring whether stress is concentrating around a region or asset group, giving investors another signal for capital withdrawal, reallocation, or entry timing.
